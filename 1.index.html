What Safety Means To Us At LET'S TREK

Our vision is very clear to me: everyone must trek. I say this because I have personally seen how trekking can transform people. It pushes you out of your comfort zone, builds confidence, teaches resilience, and often changes the way you look at yourself and the world around you. But there is one thing that must always go hand in hand with that experience — safety.

Whether someone chooses to trek with Let's Trek or independently, they should be able to return home safely after every trek. There are no two ways about it. Trekking is about adventure, exploration and stepping into the unknown, but that does not mean we should compromise on preparation, awareness or safety. The mountains deserve respect, and every trekker deserves the right systems, knowledge and support to make informed and safe decisions.

At Let's Trek, we are fortunate to be regarded across the country as one of India’s safest trekking organisations. But we don't see that as a title to be celebrated and then forgotten. We see it as a responsibility. It pushes us to constantly ask ourselves: Can we make trekking safer than it is today? Can we identify risks earlier? Can we build better systems? Can we prepare trekkers and teams better for what the mountains can throw at them?

That is why our entire team is continuously working towards understanding what the future of trekking safety should look like. We study our experiences, learn from what goes right and what goes wrong, improve our systems, train our people, and keep looking for better ways to protect trekkers. For us, safety is not just a protocol or a checklist. It is a culture — something that has to be lived and practised every single day.

And I believe this is an endless journey. Five years from now, I want you to hear me say the same thing: we are still working on making trekking safer. Because safety can never have a full stop. The moment we believe we have done enough, we stop learning. And in the mountains, that is when complacency can become dangerous. So, for me, the goal is not to reach a point where we can say, “We have achieved safety.” The goal is to keep asking better questions, keep learning, keep improving and keep raising the standard — so that every trekker, every team member and every family waiting back home can have greater confidence in the journey.

Safety has no finish line. It is a continuous quest to make every trek safer, and that is the journey we have chosen to be on.
Tulsi K Nimawat
Founder, CEO, Let's Trek
